## 2026-10-18 — Phase 3 checkpoint: the overview is polities and world events
Bob's new ideas change what the zoomed-out timeline is. Until now it was era bands, a row per region, and the "All" stop showed Bob's categories as lanes down the left. Now the top of the timeline shows **polities and world events**. Each polity is a strip whose thickness follows its power, and related polities sit together (Greece, Macedon and Persia near each other, China far off). World events (the Reformation, the split of the Roman Empire, the spread of a religion, the steam engine) are gold. Opening a polity or world event drops its lanes beneath it, and the map follows a picked polity, an open lane, or a click on an area. The full direction is in `docs/vision.md` (The overview).

Bob's choices:
- **Four lanes, not six dimensions:**
  - Politics (constitutions, laws, leaders, movements; so the "law" option he asked for is Politics, shown as "Politics and law").
  - War.
  - Economy (production and distribution).
  - Philosophy and religion (leaders, texts, schisms).
  - There's no People lane. An entry can be in several lanes. The automatic sort never overrides what Bob sets.
- **Eras become a backdrop**, a labelled wash behind their region's strips, rather than being removed or turned into groups.
- **Thickness** starts from the territory's area in each year (`areaKm2`, already stored), and Bob's relative strength points (1–5, citing a highlight) override it.

What follows:
- **Phase order.** Phase 4 is now "The overview", and the old Phase 4 (themes, influences, threads) is Phase 4b. Phase 4 opens with one prototype that also absorbs the long-range one. Polities from the tray moves from Phase 5 to Phase 4, because the strips need polities Bob can add.
- **Schema v9** holds `lanes` (several per entry and highlight), the overview mark (`overview: 'shown' | 'world'`), `strength` on territories, region parents, and territories as connection ends. The mark replaces the planned landmark star: what shows at the top is Bob's choice, and world events are a kind of mark, not a kind of entry.
- **The upgrade** fills `lanes` from `dimension` (political → politics, economic → economy, intellectual and religious → ideas). Social and technological have no lane, so those entries are left to the automatic sort, and `dimension` stays readable so nothing is lost.
- **Renumbering.** Phase 3 ticket 4 already used v8 (place dates), so the later bumps move up two: regimes v10, economies v11, institutions and reception v12, borders and forces v13.
- **The classic layout** stays until Phase 4 ticket 6 replaces the category-lane overview, then a small ticket drops it.
- **Inbox items.** Resizable panes and search are small tickets to do before Phase 4. Search folds in paused ticket 9. Box-select with bulk edit and delete waits for v9 so it can set lanes. Better highlight reading (the Laurion passage, ticking several "Becomes" candidates, filling one in by hand) is Phase 4 ticket 3, after the lanes change so its eval cases are rerun once. "Clicking an era zooms the map" was dropped: the map following a picked polity covers it.

This supersedes the level-0 half of "What's in an era" (eras are the backdrop now, and `src/model/polities.ts` will decide what's in a polity the same way) and the landmark star in "Long range: prominence, clusters, threads". Level 1's six dimension lanes become a polity's four lanes. The morph and `eraLanes` are reused.

## 2026-10-18 — Inside an era (level 1)
An era's lanes hold what level 0 counts for it: its members and its inner eras' members, plus the inner eras themselves as spans. An entry's lane is its own `dimension`, else the dimension most of its highlights have (first in pigment order on a tie), else "No dimension yet" (`entryDimensions` in `src/model/eras.ts`). That's the mirror of level 0's rule that a highlight without a dimension takes its entry's, and it keeps entries Bob added before the tray set `dimension` out of the bottom lane. Clicking a band now steps inside the era instead of opening it; the era's name in the header row above the lanes opens it, and "‹ All eras", the Eras stop, Esc or zooming out past the era's years go back. Lane counts are the era's `mix`, so they match level 0's strip. Opening is a morph over `--motion-move`: the lanes are laid out once and squeezed into the band's rectangle, growing as the window frames the era, while the other rows fade; with reduced motion only the fade remains. The timeline host carries `data-level` once nothing is moving, so tests wait on it rather than on time. The Classical Greece fixture's entries now carry the `dimension` they only had as a tag.

## 2026-10-18 — What's in an era (level 0)
Level 0 needs each era's highlights. Only periods have a `region`, so other entries can't say which region they belong to. `src/model/eras.ts` decides it. An entry is in the era it is `part of`, following part-of links up (a battle → its war → an era). Failing that, it's in the innermost era of each region whose dates hold its middle. A highlight counts for the eras of the entries it's used by, the era itself, and the era's themes. It also counts for the eras around those in the same row (the Fifty Years' highlights count for Classical Greece too). We chose "every region whose dates hold it" over "only when one region does": adding Persia's eras later then never takes highlights away from Greece. The cost is that overlapping regions share highlights that belong to one of them. Phase 4 ticket 4 (long range, with about 15 regions) is where to check this rule, since threads use the same "which era is this end in". Eras are periods (`subject: 'period'`), plus older spans with no `subject` once Bob gives them a region, so old docs don't fill "Elsewhere" with wars. Regions are ordered with the most highlighted first and "Elsewhere" last. Spectral SC (600, from `@fontsource/spectral-sc`) is bundled for era and region names. The Classical Greece fixture is now schema 8: its eras are periods in a Greece row, and its highlights and the Classical period's themes moved from `next.json` into the doc.

## 2026-10-18 — Long range: prominence, clusters, threads
Bob asked how the timeline stays clear and good-looking zoomed out once he has added thousands of things, and how he'll see connections across eras at that range. Speed isn't the issue (the spike's layout handles 50,000 entries). The plan had two gaps. Level 0 showed only era bands, so wars, people and events vanished. Connections were drawn only inside one era. Bob chose: **threads on focus** (pick an era, landmark or theme and its links light up across eras and regions as bundled ribbons), with a **"Show all threads"** setting for the whole web drawn faintly. Also landmarks in bands, clusters that split as you zoom, lenses, and collapsible regions. Not chosen: following one thread as a single line (Phase 5c's reception threads cover that for ideas), and a separate connection graph view as the main answer.

What counts as prominent comes from Bob's own work: highlights, connections, notes, and a star he can set. It never comes from an outside ranking, since the app ships no content and the point is what *he* learned. Threads default to on-focus because a web always drawn at full strength is clutter. Phase 3's renderer leaves room: clusters in the layout (ticket 2), and a landmark row and gutters at level 0 (ticket 5). Phase 4 opens with a prototype on a scaled-up fixture. The star and region parents are schema; the prototype decides whether they join v8.

## 2026-10-17 — Drafting from a highlight: quotes checked in code, the article cached

## 2026-10-17 — Subjects: nearest class wins, walked with the query service
`src/model/subjects.ts` classifies an article's Wikidata item by its own classes (P31, plus P279 for types of thing like the trireme), walking P279 up at most four levels. The nearest class Chronicle knows wins; ties go by a fixed order (person, battle, war, migration, polity, period, event, technology, idea, economy, work, place), so a polis that is also an archaeological site is a polity. Wikidata's upper classes are tangled (modern Athens is a "free city", hence a state), so the anchor table lists a few direct classes ("big city") alongside broad ones. Parents come from `query.wikidata.org` (one small SPARQL query per level, usually none), because the API would send each class's every claim. Subjects are cached in the article cache, not the doc. Alliances (the Delian League) are polities until Phase 5 gives them their own kind. Fixtures are recorded with `scripts/record-subjects.mjs`; Wikidata rate-limits fast, so it pauses between requests.

## 2026-10-17 — Spikes: an SVG timeline of our own, a reader with quote anchors, map motion without flubber
Three spikes ran in worktrees (reports in `docs/spikes/`, code on the unmerged branches `spike/timeline`, `spike/reader` and `spike/map-motion`). Numbers are from an M2 with a Pixel 7 profile at 4× CPU; real phones are checked before each build ticket.
**Timeline (Phase 3).** The renderer is a pure layout (entries, window, size, level → a culled list of marks) plus a painter. It paints SVG, with our own Pointer Events driver (about 90 lines) rather than d3-zoom, which covered three of five gestures and needed overrides. Time is decimal years, never `Date`, so the ±271k-year limit goes (ticks work from 3 million BCE down to days). At 2,000 entries SVG, canvas and a hybrid all held 60 fps, so SVG wins on CSS theming, crisp text and drag handles; at 50,000 SVG still held while canvas fell to 15–20 fps (possibly a software-raster artifact). SVG started 2–3× faster than vis-timeline with less memory, and dropping vis-timeline, vis-data and moment saves about 165 KB gzipped. Canvas stays possible because only the painter would change. Keyboard and screen readers use one roving option node with `aria-activedescendant`.
**Reader (Phase 1).** Articles come from `/w/rest.php/v1/page/{title}/html` (and `/revision/{id}/html`): RESTBase's `rest_v1` already redirects there. The revision id is read from the HTML's `about` attribute, since the header isn't exposed cross-origin. HTML is cleaned by our own allowlist walker (3 KB); DOMPurify on top changed nothing, and the Sanitizer API is missing in Safari. Highlights anchor by quote (`exact`, `prefix`, `suffix`, section heading, a position hint) over text with footnote markers removed, re-anchored exact-in-section → exact-in-article → fuzzy (`approx-string-match`, MIT, 1 KB), and flagged `uncertain` when the match left its section with weak context. Over five articles: 99% found after a week, 90.5% after five years, with every wrong placement caught by the flag. Marks use the CSS Custom Highlight API (no DOM rewriting; wrapping in `<mark>` stalled phone taps up to 150 ms), with a separate description for screen readers. Tap-a-sentence is `Intl.Segmenter` plus an abbreviation merge. Cleaned articles (25–42 KB gzipped) are cached in IndexedDB with revision, page id and fetch date; images reuse the service worker's Commons cache. Fetches are serial: Wikipedia returned 429s at about 3 a second.
**Map motion (Phase 5).** Borders morph with our own code (arc-length resampling, best rotation, greedy polygon matching, holes matched or collapsed; about 2 KB): flubber twisted in 16–19 of 21 frames, took up to 1.7 s per pair on the phone, and handles neither matching nor holes. Crossfade is the fallback. A morph is a pure function of the year, about 0.7 years long (1 s at 1×), **centred on the snapshot's start year** and capped at the gap between snapshots. Routes are ribbon polygons built in pure code (band and core as two polygons, the head in its own source), sliced at their progress with `setData` only while drawing; finished routes move to a static source and fade through feature-state. `line-gradient` can't taper or dash, and `line-trim-offset` isn't in MapLibre 6.11.2. Ribbon corners need a miter limit (the spike's sharp turns flared). Battles ring with HTML markers and CSS animation. MapLibre's re-tiling, not JS, is the ceiling: about 50 territories morphing at once is the phone's limit. One clock (`{year, playing, speed}`, one rAF loop) feeds React through `useYearClock(selector)` and the map and playhead through `onFrame`.
**Playback eases while a route draws, superseding part of "Steady playback".** At 0.7 years a second a route drew in under half a second, too fast to read its name and size, so the year slows while a route is drawing and resumes its even pace after. The camera still doesn't follow.

## 2026-10-15 — Migration flows: a `Flow` record, drawn as moving arrows
Schema 4 adds `flows`: a cause, a measure (`moved` per year or `living` abroad), routes between two ends, and per-route points. An end is a present-day country (ISO-3, placed at the middle of its largest piece in the bundled `countries.json`, loaded by `data/countries.ts`), one of Bob's territories (its label point in the year shown), or a named lon/lat, so ancient migrations need no dataset. Routes are great-circle arcs bowed to the right of travel, so A → B and B → A stay apart (`ui/mapData.ts`, tested). Width is linear in the rate, scaled to the largest value ever recorded for that measure, so it stays comparable as the year plays. Cause colors are fixed pigments, not categories; categories still hide flows like anything else. Moving dashes are short line segments recomputed about 30 times a second from a pure function (`flowDashes`), because `line-dasharray` can't vary its speed per feature; with reduced motion they stand still. Arrowheads are one SDF icon drawn in code (no sprite server), so any color works. The alliance ticket's schema bump becomes 5.

## 2026-10-15 — Find a trend: a bundled catalogue, numbers fetched live

It is committed and precached, so search works offline, and it's rerun by hand now and then. It lists datasets; it isn't content, and the app still ships no history. Numbers are fetched only when Bob picks a dataset, and they're cached NetworkFirst. Both sources allow cross-origin requests. World Bank results become the same `TrendTable` as OWID CSVs, so one chooser serves both; WB aggregates (listed in the index) are choosable by name but never shade the map. No schema change: a found trend is an ordinary `Series` with `source`. OWID writes BCE years as plain negatives (-10000 = 10,000 BCE), so `fromOwidYear` in `dates.ts` shifts them by one; trends imported before this fix keep their one-year offset (invisible at those scales). Next: migration as flows between places (arrows by rate and cause, a new `Flow` record, since flows have two ends and a cause that a `Series` can't hold), fed by Bob's own historical migrations, UNHCR refugees (cause known, 1951 on) and Abel & Cohen bilateral flows (1990–2020, all reasons). UN DESA migrant stocks were dropped: they count people living abroad, not people moving. Then bubbles for counts.

## 2026-10-15 — A stable app, a manual in it, and data that carries forward
`main` is Bob's stable app on GitHub Pages and deploys only after the full check (including e2e) passes; tickets live on branches and are released when Bob says so. The dev server runs on localhost, a different origin, so its IndexedDB test data never touches his real timeline.
Saved docs used to load without migration (only imports ran `parseDoc`'s migrations). Now `openStoredDoc` upgrades them on open, after keeping an untouched copy in a `backups` store (last 5). A doc from a newer schema opens read-only, and `IdbStore.save` refuses to write over one, so a stale tab can't drop fields it doesn't know. The service worker uses `prompt`, not `autoUpdate`: a banner offers **Reload** (after flushing the save) instead of reloading mid-edit.
The manual is `docs/manual.md`, bundled with `?raw` and rendered by a small Markdown subset (`ui/markdown.ts`, no HTML strings, web and anchor links only) in a Help panel that works offline. `scripts/check-manual.mjs` fails `check` on a branch that changes `src/` (app code) without it; "Manual: none" in a commit message is the escape hatch. Help shows the build's commit and date (`__APP_VERSION__`).

## 2026-10-15 — Borders Bob draws or brings: city-states without a dataset
There is no open dataset of Greek city-state borders over time, and historical-basemaps has only three maps for Classical Greece (500, 400 and 323 BCE), at a resolution where poleis vanish. So Bob can draw a territory on the map (click points; extra parts for islands; drawing logic in the pure `ui/drawing.ts`) or bring areas from a GeoJSON file (geojson.io, QGIS, a paper's data). Both work offline and need no schema change: a drawn shape is just a `TerritorySnapshot` with dates he types. User files are simplified at a fine level (0.001°, 4 decimals) so small poleis and islands survive; historical-basemaps imports keep 0.05°. Snapshot dates are now editable, so borders can change at any year, not only at dataset years. The app still ships no borders of its own.

## 2026-10-15 — Map: MapLibre behind an adapter, no tiles, borders Bob chooses
The map (MapLibre GL 6, pinned) follows the vis-timeline rule: only `src/ui/MapView.tsx` imports it, and the logic that decides what is on the map in a year lives in pure modules (`model/atYear.ts`, `ui/mapData.ts`). There is no tile server: a simplified Natural Earth basemap (countries, lakes, rivers, about 1 MB) is bundled in `public/geo` and precached, so the map works offline. Labels are HTML markers, because symbol text needs a glyph server.
Borders come from historical-basemaps (GPL-3.0, credited on each territory). Bob imports the polities he studies; each import is simplified (Douglas–Peucker, 0.05°) so backups stay small, and it can extend an existing territory (Roman Republic → Roman Empire). The full world maps appear only as an opt-in "reference borders" backdrop, which is fetched, cached by the service worker, and never written to the doc, so the app still ships no history.
Trends gain `regions` (ISO-3 codes or territory ids). Countries are shaded on today's borders, as Our World in Data does, and the legend says so; territory values are circles. The color scale is fixed over all years so change over time is visible. "Tech spread" is the map with past entries kept and one category shown; a separate tech tree stays in M5.

## 2026-10-15 — Sync: one person, many devices → per-record last-writer-wins
"Online editing" means Bob on his own devices, not collaboration. Every record has an `updatedAt` and deletions leave tombstones (`doc.deleted`), so `mergeDocs` can combine two copies safely. CRDTs (Yjs/Automerge) were considered and rejected as unnecessary complexity for one editor. Interim sync is export on one device, then **Import → Merge** on the other. Automatic sync (see ROADMAP) will reuse `mergeDocs` against a remote copy.

## 2026-10-15 — Local-first PWA
React, TypeScript, Vite and vite-plugin-pwa, with IndexedDB via `idb`. Works fully offline once loaded. Hosting is static (GitHub Pages). Persistence goes through the `DocStore` interface, so sync wraps a store instead of changing callers.

## 2026-10-15 — vis-timeline behind an adapter
Mature, editable, and supports lanes. Only `src/ui/TimelineView.tsx` imports it. Known limit: JS Dates reach about ±271,000 years, enough for human history but not deep time. A custom renderer would replace the adapter if that's ever needed.

## 2026-10-15 — Trends are our own SVG, not vis Graph2d
Graph2d has no log scale and styles poorly. Trend strips are small SVGs that read the timeline's visible window, so they pan and zoom with it. Each strip has its own y-scale: they show shape, not cross-series magnitude.

## 2026-10-15 — Astronomical year numbering internally
431 BCE is stored as -430, so arithmetic just works. Display and input happen only in `dates.ts`. Typing "-431" means 431 BCE (the historian's sense), not ISO's astronomical meaning. Axis ticks at decade-or-wider zoom show round numbers ("500 BCE" for tick -500), accepting a one-year offset that is invisible at that scale.

## 2026-10-15 — Fonts bundled, not from Google Fonts
Spectral (reading text) and Public Sans (interface) come from `@fontsource` so they work offline.
