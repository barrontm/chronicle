# Chronicle manual

Chronicle is your own history timeline. It starts empty: you fill it in as you read and study, and it helps you connect what you learn and find it again. Everything stays on your device.

## Getting started

- Chronicle opens with the article you're reading beside the map, and the timeline under the map (see [The reader layout](#the-reader-layout)). **Open an article** (top bar) finds one on Wikipedia, and highlighting it is how you add things. Double-click the timeline to add something at that date.
- Prefer the older arrangement? **More → Use the classic layout** brings back **Add** (an event, a period, a person, and the rest) and the **Timeline**, **Map** and **Both** views. Choose it again to go back. Both layouts draw the same timeline, and everything you've saved is the same in either.
- **Library** lists your notes, sources, trends and territories. **Help** opens this manual. **Search** finds anything you've saved (see [Finding things](#finding-things)).
- The top bar says **Saved on this device** once your changes are stored. There is no account and no cloud copy, so export a backup now and then (see [Backups and working offline](#backups-and-working-offline)).

> Install Chronicle like an app: in Chrome or Edge use **Install** in the address bar; on iPhone use **Share → Add to Home Screen**; on Android use **Add to home screen**. It then opens in its own window and works offline.

## Entries: events, periods and people

- An **event** is a moment (a battle, a treaty). A **period** has a start and an end (a war, a reign). A **person** runs from birth to death.
- Type dates the way you would say them: `431 BCE`, `c. 1440`, `1914-07-28`. The field shows how it read your date. Leave the end empty if it's unknown or ongoing.
- Approximate dates (`c.`) show with a dashed edge on the timeline.
- Zoomed far out, a name that doesn't fit is shortened or left off; point at the entry to see it, or zoom in. A name near the right edge moves to the left of its dot so it stays on screen.
- Each entry has a **category** (politics, war, ideas, science, technology, society, arts). The colored chips under the top bar show or hide a category everywhere, on the timeline and on the map.
- **Kind of thing** says what an entry is: a person, a war, a battle, a period, an idea, a place and so on. The timeline draws each kind its own way (see [The timeline](#the-timeline)). Entries added from Wikipedia or the reader fill it in for you; you can change it, or leave it as **Not set** and the entry is drawn as an event, period or person, as it is now.
- A **place** (a city, a site) runs from when it was founded to when it was abandoned. Either date can be left empty: one still there has no end, and one whose founding nobody knows is on the map in every year but not on the timeline. Its pin fades in over a few years from its founding, and out after it was abandoned.
- A period also has a **Region**, such as Greece or China: the row it sits in at **Eras** on the timeline. Type your own, or pick one you've used before. From Wikipedia, a period's region starts as its country or continent, spelled the way you already spell it.
- Click an entry to open it, then **Edit** to change it or **Show on map** to jump to its year on the map.

### Moving through time

- **Scroll** with the mouse wheel or swipe sideways on a trackpad to move back and forth through time. Hold **Shift** while scrolling to move up and down through the categories instead.
- **Zoom** with **Ctrl** or **⌘** plus the wheel, or pinch on a trackpad or phone.
- The **thumbwheel** under the timeline spins through time: drag it, or flick it and let it glide. The year in the middle of the timeline shows beside it. With the thumbwheel selected, the arrow keys step a little and Page Up / Page Down a whole screen.
- The **zoom bar** beside it has **−** and **+**, a slider, and stops for a **Year**, a **Decade**, a **Century** and a **Millennium**. **All** fits everything on your timeline. It also says how much time is in view.
- **Click an entry** and the timeline zooms to its time, with everything it's connected to in view. If its connections are centuries away, it zooms to the entry alone. **Back to where I was** (top right of the timeline) returns to where you were before. **Show their time** (or **Show its time**) in the entry zooms there again.

### Places

An entry's places put it on the map as a pin: where a battle was fought, or where a person was born and died. Entries added from Wikipedia usually come with them; for anything else, add them yourself while editing the entry.

1. Under **Places**, press **Add a place** and type its name.
2. Find where it is in one of three ways:
   - **Look up** (or press Enter) searches Wikipedia for places with that name. Pick one and its coordinates are filled in. Your name for the place stays as you typed it. This needs a connection.
   - **Pick on the map** and click the spot.
   - Type the **coordinates**, latitude first, the way Wikipedia or an atlas prints them: `37.97, 23.72` or `37°58′N 23°43′E`. Underneath, it shows how it read them.
3. For a person, choose what happened there: **Born here**, **Died here** or another place in their life.
4. Press **Add place**, then **Add to timeline** (or **Save changes**) to keep the entry.

**Edit** changes a place and **Remove** takes it off. An entry can have as many places as you like.

## The reader layout

Reading is how you add things, so Chronicle opens with the reader. (If you'd turned on "Try the new layout" before, this is it, now for everyone. **More → Use the classic layout** switches back to the older arrangement.)

- On a laptop, the article you're reading sits beside the map, with the timeline under the map.
- On a phone, the article fills the screen. **Map** opens the map and timeline, and **Back to reading** returns to the article.
- **Open an article** (top bar) searches Wikipedia and opens the whole article in the reader, with its pictures and footnotes.
- Links in the article open in the reader too. **Back** returns to the article you came from, and the **reading trail** in the top bar lists the articles you've opened, so you can click back to any of them.
- Under the title, the reader says what the article is about, from Wikidata: a person, war, battle, polity, period, event, movement or idea, technology, economy, work, place or migration, with its dates and what it was part of ("Battle · 480 BCE · part of Second Persian invasion of Greece"). If you already have it as an entry or a territory, it says **Already in Chronicle**; click that to open it. Some articles aren't about one thing, and then the line stays empty.
- An article's infobox (the box of facts Wikipedia puts at the top) starts folded to one line, with its picture as a small thumbnail, so the text starts near the top. Click **Show** to open it and **Hide** to fold it again; Chronicle remembers your choice on this device. You can highlight its rows like any other text.
- Articles you've opened are kept on this device, so you can reread them offline. Opening a new one needs a connection. The line at the end of each article says which edit of the Wikipedia page you're reading.
- The colored category chips above the map show or hide a category on the map and the timeline.

### Highlighting

- **On a laptop**, select some words and press **Highlight** (it appears just above them) or the **H** key.
- **On a phone**, select words and press **Highlight** at the bottom of the screen, or just tap a sentence to highlight all of it. Tapping the next sentence adds it to the same highlight.
- A highlight is saved the moment you make it, and the article becomes one of your sources (**Library → Sources**). Highlights are quotes from the article, kept apart from your own notes.
- The highlight you just made is shaded darker, and a tray slides up under the text with what you can do with it. The article scrolls so the passage stays above the tray. Click or tap a highlight again to open its tray.
- The tray shows the quote and its section, then **Becomes**: what the highlight could be about. The article's own subject comes first, then each article the passage links to, in the order you read them. Each says what kind of thing it is (a war, a person, a polity), and **In Chronicle** or **Linked** if you already have it. Pick one; the first war, battle or event the passage links is picked for you, otherwise the article's subject. What you pick is remembered with the highlight, and the tray still shows the list offline once it's been looked up.
- Under your pick are its facts from Wikidata: when, where, what it was part of, who took part. Underlined names are already in Chronicle.
- If what you picked isn't in Chronicle yet and Wikidata says where it was (a battle, a city), it shows on the map as a dashed umber ghost with a "?". Pick something else and the ghost follows; add it and it becomes a real pin.
- Dates follow Wikipedia: from 1583 on they're New Style (Gregorian), before that Old Style (Julian). So Newton is born 4 January 1643, not 25 December 1642.
- A language the passage links (Latin, Ancient Greek) is never offered as an event, even though Wikidata dates it. Highlighting Justinian's dates opens on Justinian, not on Latin.
- Chronicle remembers what pages are about on your device. When a new version reads Wikidata better, it looks them up again the next time you're online.
- **Dimension**: pick political, economic, social, technological, intellectual or religious. The highlight takes that color; pick it again to clear it.
- **Add to map and timeline** fills in an entry for what you picked from its Wikipedia article, with your highlight as its quote and the article you were reading as its source. Check it and press **Add to timeline**. The highlight is then underlined, and it shows on the entry. If you already have it, the button says **Link to …** instead and adds the highlight to it straight away. Either way, connections Wikipedia knows of to your other entries are suggested (see Connections from Wikipedia).
- **Places** can be added from the tray like anything else: Athens becomes a place with its pin on the map, and its founding date from Wikidata if it has one. Polities and ideas can't be added from the tray yet; it says so, and you can keep the highlight or ask Claude.
- **Ask Claude** is for what has no article of its own: Pericles' decree of 449 BC, the tribute that paid for the Parthenon. Claude reads the passage and its section and drafts what they state: entries with their dates (and a place, looked up on Wikipedia), and connections with their effect ("Delian League *funded* the decree"). Under each draft are the words of the passage it rests on. It also suggests a dimension, marked with a dashed ring in the picker; it's only used if you pick it. Asking needs a connection and your Anthropic API key (see Drafting with AI); it usually costs a few cents.
- Claude's drafts wait in the tray, dashed. While the highlight is open, drafted entries show as dashed umber ghosts with a "?" on the map (if they have a place) and on the timeline (if they have a date), and drafted connections show on the timeline as dashed umber arrows labelled with their effect and a "?" ("funded ?"). Once you accept them they're drawn like the rest. **Accept** adds a draft, with the highlight as its evidence, and moves the map to its year. **Edit** opens an entry in the editor first. **Reject** leaves it out. **Undo** takes back either choice; undoing an entry also takes back the connections you accepted to it. A connection to a drafted entry waits until you accept the entry.
- The drafts stay with the highlight on this device until you decide, even if you close the tray or reload. **Discard the rest** throws away the ones you haven't decided on.
- **Just keep the highlight** closes the tray and keeps it as a plain highlight (once the highlight is on something, it says **Done**). **Remove** deletes it. Pressing **Esc**, clicking elsewhere or opening another article also keeps it.
- With nothing open, the tray counts the article's highlights and how many are on your map and timeline.
- **Library → Highlights** lists all your highlights under the article or book they came from, with their dimension, the entries and connections they're on, and how many of Claude's drafts are waiting. An entry's panel shows the highlights behind it.
- Wikipedia articles change. When you reopen one, Chronicle finds your highlights in the new version, even when the words around them were edited. A dotted underline means it found something close but isn't sure it's the same passage. If a passage was deleted, the article says how many highlights it couldn't find; they're still saved.

## Finding things

- **Search** in the top bar looks through everything you've saved: entries, territories, notes, sources and highlights. On a laptop, press **/** to jump to it. On a phone, tap the magnifier, and **Cancel** closes it.
- Capitals and accents don't matter: "pericles" finds Périclès. Every word you type has to be there.
- Things whose name matches come first, then things that only mention your words somewhere: an entry's description, a note, a source's authors or remarks, or a highlight's text. You see up to 20 results. Each has its kind's mark (or says Note, Source or Highlight) and its dates, and the words around the match when it's only mentioned.
- Use the arrow keys and **Enter**, or click a result. An entry opens and the timeline moves to it, and the map goes to its year and its place. A territory opens and the map shows all of it in its first year. A note or a source opens in the side panel. A highlight opens the entry it's on, or the highlights in the Library if it isn't on one yet.
- Search works offline: everything it looks through is on your device.

## The timeline

Both layouts draw the same timeline.

- Drag it, or scroll, to move through time; hold **Shift** and scroll to move up and down the lanes; hold **Ctrl** or **⌘** and scroll, or pinch, to zoom. It zooms out as far as millions of years and in as close as days. Click an entry to open it, and double-click (or double-tap) an empty spot to add one there.
- In each lane, events are dots along the top, with periods and then lives in rows below.
- **Each kind of thing has its own shape**, the same one the tray shows:
  - an event is a dot; a battle a circled ×; a technology a cog; a work (a book, a play, a building) a page, joined by a thin line to the life of the person who made it, when they're connected;
  - a period is a plain bar; a war a bar with a tick for each battle that's part of it (a battle with no war in Chronicle is a point of its own); a polity a bar starting with a solid square; an economy a bar starting with a stack of coins; a migration a bar ending in an arrow, starting with chevrons;
  - a movement or idea is a thin thread starting with a spark; a place a thin ground line with a pin standing at its founding;
  - a person is a rounded lifeline.
  Click a battle's tick to open the battle. An entry whose kind of thing is **Not set** is drawn as an event, period or person. When entries are too close together to tell apart, they merge into a badge with a count, named after the one you've highlighted most. Click the badge to zoom in until they separate. Point at anything to see its name and dates.
- **Open an entry** and its connections light up: amber for who influenced it, blue for whom it influenced, grey dashed for other connections, with arrows. Everything else fades back. If a connected entry is in a lane scrolled out of view, its arrow runs off the edge toward it.
- **Drag an open entry to change its dates.** Click it first, then drag its bar or dot: both dates move together and it keeps its length. On a period or a life, drag either end to change just that date. The new dates show above the timeline while you drag. Dates move in whole years, months or days, as precisely as you entered them, so 431 BCE stays a year and 15 June 1215 stays a day. Afterwards a note says where it went, with **Undo**. Dragging anything that isn't open still moves through time, so you can't move an entry by accident.
- **Eras** (the first stop on the zoom bar) shows your periods as eras, with a row for each region, the region you've highlighted most at the top. A period without a region goes in **Elsewhere**, at the bottom; an older period whose kind of thing is **Not set** shows once you give it a region. Each row says how many of your highlights fall in its eras. Each era is a block with its name and dates; a period inside another one (the Fifty Years inside Classical Greece) sits on a row under it. A dashed brown edge means that date is approximate. The colored strip along the bottom shows what your highlights in that era are about, in the reader's colors: blue political, gold economic, rose social, green technological, violet intellectual, red religious. Point at an era to see the numbers. The small shapes inside an era are the entries you've highlighted most. Click an era to step inside it (see below), or one of its shapes to open that entry among the others. An era's highlights are its own, its themes', and those of everything inside it: what's part of it, otherwise whatever falls in its dates. Drag, scroll and zoom as usual; **Year**, **Decade**, **Century**, **Millennium** or **All** go back to your entries. On a phone, each region's name sits above its row.
- **Inside an era.** Click an era, or zoom in over it (Ctrl or ⌘ and scroll, or pinch), and it opens into six lanes, one for each kind of history, in the reader's colors: political, economic, social, technological, intellectual, religious. Each lane says how many of your highlights are about it. Periods and lives are named bars, dashed where a date is approximate; events are diamonds on a row above them. An entry sits in the lane of its dimension (the one you picked when you highlighted it, or the one most of its highlights have); entries with none yet get a lane of their own at the bottom, **No dimension yet**. A lane with nothing in it says so. The era's name sits above the lanes: click it to open the era itself. Click a bar or diamond to open that entry; Shift and scroll (or drag) moves up and down the lanes. **‹ All eras**, **Eras**, **Esc**, or zooming back out takes you back to all the eras. On a phone, each lane shows its bars without names, then a list of what's in it with dates; tap one to open it.
- **With the keyboard**, click the timeline or tab to it, then use **←** and **→** to go from entry to entry in a lane, **↑** and **↓** to go to the lane above or below, and **Home** and **End** for a lane's first and last. **Enter** opens the entry and **Esc** closes it. **Shift** with **←** or **→** moves through time, **+** and **−** zoom, and **Alt** with **←** or **→** moves the open entry's dates a little. Screen readers say each entry's name, dates and lane as you go. At **Eras**, the arrow keys go from era to era in a region and up and down between regions, and **Enter** steps inside one. Inside an era, they go along a lane and up and down between lanes.

## Adding from Wikipedia

**Add → From Wikipedia…** searches Wikipedia. Pick a page and you get a new entry filled in: kind, what kind of thing it is, and dates from Wikidata, places for the map, a picture with its credit, the intro as a quotation, and the page as a source. Nothing is saved until you check it and press **Add to timeline**. If you already have that entry, Chronicle tells you.

The intro is kept as a **highlight**: a passage quoted from its source, shown on the entry and never mixed with your own notes. Quotes saved by earlier versions of Chronicle become highlights too when you open or import them.

This needs a connection.

## Drafting with AI

**Add → Draft with AI…** lets Claude do the searching for you. Say what you're studying ("the main battles of the Greco-Persian Wars") and Claude picks the Wikipedia pages that belong, along with a category and a one-line reason for each. Then Chronicle reads the facts from Wikipedia and Wikidata, the same way **From Wikipedia** does: dates, places, the picture, the intro as a quotation, and the page as a source. Claude chooses and connects; it never writes the dates or places.

Nothing is added until you decide, one record at a time:

- **Accept** adds the entry and its source (**Undo** takes it back). **Edit** opens it in the editor so you can change it before adding it. **Reject** leaves it out (**Undo** brings it back).
- Entries waiting for you show on the map as **dashed pins** in their category's color, whatever year the map is on. **Show on map** takes you there.
- **Connections** Claude suggests (a battle is part of its war) can be accepted once both entries are on your timeline.
- **Left out by the checker** lists what Chronicle refused and why: a page Claude didn't actually find, something already on your timeline, too many at once.
- An accepted entry says **Drafted with AI** at the top, with the model and prompt version, so you can always tell it apart. Claude's reasons are shown while you review and are never saved, and drafting never writes notes or makes pictures.

While reading, you can also ask Claude about a passage you highlighted (see Highlighting).

It needs a connection and your own **Anthropic API key** (from console.anthropic.com), which you paste in the panel once (the reader's tray links to it). The key stays in this browser only: it's never in your backups or exports, and **Forget** removes it. Drafts are billed to your Anthropic account, usually a few cents each.

## Notes, sources and citations

- **Write a note** on any entry, territory or trend. A note can be linked to several things at once, so one note can tie Pericles, the Delian League and Athens together.
- **Sources** are your bibliography: books, articles, web pages. Add them from **Library → Sources → New source**.
- **Add and cite** in an entry or note links a source, with the pages or location you read.
- An entry's **Bibliography** gathers what to read on it: sources it cites, then sources cited by notes about it and by entries connected to it (each says where it was cited). For a person it also lists **Their works**: every source with them as an author. Write the author as you like (`Plato`, `Darwin, Charles`, `Plato; trans. Grube`) and it's found.

## Connections

In an entry's editor, connect it to another entry and choose the kind: *influenced*, *caused*, *part of*, *responded to* or *related*. Connections show on both entries. On the map, **Layers → Show connections** draws a dashed line between connected entries when both are on the map.

> Use *part of* for battles in a war, so later you can see a war and its battles together.

### Connections from Wikipedia

When you add something from Wikipedia, Chronicle checks what Wikidata says about it against the entries you already have, and suggests connections under **Wikipedia also says** in the editor:

- *part of*: the Battle of Salamis is part of the Second Persian invasion, if you have the invasion.
- *led to*: what it came after, and what came after it.
- *influenced*: for people, who influenced them and whom they influenced.
- *related*: who took part, and where it happened, when those are entries of yours.

They're ticked, so they're saved with the entry. Untick any you disagree with first. If you added it from a highlight, the highlight is kept as the connection's evidence, and it shows under the connection on both entries.

When you **Link** a highlight in the reader to an entry you already have, the tray lists the same kind of connections for that entry, ticked, and saves them when you link.

### Influences

Click a person on the timeline and the people connected to them by *influenced* light up: amber for who influenced them, blue for whom they influenced, with arrows showing which way it went. Entries connected in other ways (*caused*, *part of*, *responded to*, *related*) get a grey outline and a dashed line. Everything else fades back. The timeline zooms so they're all in view. Click empty space or close the panel to clear it. Lines only show while both entries are in view.

For a person added from Wikipedia, **Suggest influences** lists who Wikipedia says influenced them and whom they influenced. People already on your timeline get **Connect**; the rest get **Add from Wikipedia**. When you add a person from Wikipedia, their influences who are already on your timeline are suggested as connections (see Connections from Wikipedia). Suggestions need a connection.

## Pictures

**Add a picture** on an entry. Search Wikimedia Commons (credit and license come with it), paste an **Image link**, or **Upload** your own. Pictures you have looked at stay available offline.

## Trends over time

A trend is a number that changes over the years (population, the price of wheat), drawn as a strip under the timeline.

- **Add → Trend over time**, then paste one “year, value” per line, or bring numbers in from a dataset.
- Choose **Linear** or **Log** scale. Log suits numbers that grow by multiples.

### Find a trend

A new trend opens with **Find a trend**: one search over about 4,200 Our World in Data charts and about 1,500 World Bank indicators (population, the economy, migration, health, education, technology).

- Type a few words, like “population”, “GDP per capita” or “migrant women”. Every word has to match, and words in the title count most.
- **From** narrows the search to one source. **Goes back before** keeps only data that starts earlier than a year, like 1800 or 500 BCE. Our World in Data has the long histories (population back to 10,000 BCE, GDP back to year 1); World Bank data mostly starts in 1960.
- Pick a result, then choose a country, the World, or **Every country**, and press **Use**. The name, unit and source are filled in for you, and anything you've already typed is kept.
- **Every country** lets you color the map by the trend (see [The map](#the-map)).
- You can also paste an Our World in Data chart link.
- Searching works offline. Getting a trend's numbers needs a connection, but a trend you've fetched before also comes back offline.

## The map

- The map and the timeline share one **year**. Type a year in the year box (like `490 BCE`), drag the slider, or press **Play** to move through time. Choose the speed next to it.
- Entries with places show as **pins** while the year is within their dates. Events stay for a few decades afterwards so you can see them. **Layers → Keep past entries on the map** leaves them all, to watch something spread.
- **Layers → Show reference borders** draws faint outlines of every polity from the nearest historical world map. They're for orientation only, aren't saved, and need a connection the first time.
- **Layers → Color the map by** shades countries by a trend for the current year, on today's borders, with a legend.
- **Layers → Migration** shows the migrations you've recorded as arrows, and lets you hide some causes. See [Migration](#migration).
- The map remembers where you left it on this device.

## Territories

A territory is a polity's borders over time: a city-state, a kingdom, an empire. Each set of borders has dates, and the map shows whichever set is in force for the year.

**Add a territory** on the map (or **Add → Territory on the map…**) offers three ways:

### Draw your own

For city-states and anything the historical maps don't have. Works offline.

1. Press **Draw on the map**. Zoom in first if you like; the map's own buttons and scrolling still work.
2. Click around the border, point by point.
3. Click the first point again to close the outline, or press **Done**.
4. For a separate piece, like an island, press **Add another part** and outline it too.
5. **Undo point** removes the last point; **Cancel** or **Esc** stops without saving.
6. Give it a name, a category, and **From** and **Until** dates. Leave **Until** empty if the borders last until your next set (or for good).

### From a GeoJSON file

If you have borders from geojson.io, QGIS or a dataset, choose the file under **Or bring borders from a GeoJSON file**. If the file holds several areas, pick one. Its name fills in if the file has one. Fine detail is kept, so small city-states and islands survive.

### From historical maps

Pick a **Map year**, press **Show its polities**, choose one, and Chronicle looks for it across a range of world maps. Each map's borders last until the next map's year. These maps are approximate and coarse for the ancient world (for Classical Greece there are only maps for 500, 400 and 323 BCE), and they need a connection.

### Borders that change over time

Open a territory and press **Edit**. Under **Borders over time**, every set of borders has its own **From** and **Until** dates, which you can change. **Draw borders** or a GeoJSON file adds another set, for example Athens after 338 BCE. **Remove** deletes a set. Press **Save changes** when you're done.

## Migration

A migration is people moving between places: the Irish leaving during the famine, Greek colonists sailing west, the Great Migration north. On the map it's a curved arrow from where they left to where they went, **thicker for more people** and **colored by cause**. Hover over an arrow to read it, for example "Ireland → United States of America · famine & disaster · about 130,000 a year (1846–1855)". Click it to open the migration.

To record one, choose **Add → Migration on the map…** (or **New migration** in the Library):

1. Give it a **Name** and a **Cause**: conflict & persecution, work & economy, famine & disaster, forced (slavery, deportation), colonization & settlement, family & other, or unknown.
2. Choose what **the numbers are**:
   - **People moving each year**: dashes move along the arrow toward where people went, faster when more people are moving.
   - **People living abroad**: how many people from one place lived in the other that year. These are still arrows, so they're never mistaken for people on the move.
3. For each route, pick **From** and **To**. Each end can be **a country** (today's countries, by name), **one of your territories** (drawn where its borders are in the year shown), or **a place on the map**: give it a name and press **Pick on the map**, or type its latitude and longitude. Ancient and early modern places work this way.
4. Type the numbers one year per line, like `1846, 130000`. Years in between are filled in, and the arrow only shows between the first and last year. For a steady rate, give the same number for the first and last year. Estimates are fine; say how sure you are in the **Note**.
5. **Add another route** for the same migration going to more places (Ireland to Britain, to Canada…).
6. Add the **Source** of the numbers and link the **Entry on your timeline** it belongs to. That entry then lists it under **On the map**.

The map's legend shows the causes on the map that year and how many routes are drawn. If you delete a territory that a route uses, the route keeps that end as a named place where the territory was.

## Backups and working offline

- **More → Export a backup** downloads your whole timeline as one file. Keep it somewhere safe; it's also how you move your timeline to another device.
- **More → Import a file…** opens a backup. If you already have a timeline, choose **Merge into my timeline** (keeps both; where the same thing changed in both, the newer change wins) or **Replace my timeline**.
- Everything except searching Wikipedia and Commons, getting a trend's numbers, and loading historical maps, works offline. Those say so when you're offline.
- Older backups always open in newer versions of Chronicle.

## Versions

There are two copies of Chronicle:

- **Your app**, at the web address you installed it from (GitHub Pages). This is the stable version you use day to day, and your timeline lives there.
- **The version in progress**, which runs on your computer while Claude Code works on the next change. It keeps its own separate test data, so nothing there touches your timeline.

When a change is finished and tested, it is released to your app. The next time you open it, a banner says **A new version of Chronicle is ready**. It never reloads by itself, so finish what you're doing and press **Reload**; your work is saved first.

Your data carries into new versions:

- When a new version changes how data is stored, your timeline is upgraded automatically when you open it, and an untouched copy of the old data is kept on your device.
- If an old tab or old copy of the app opens data saved by a newer version, it shows **Read-only** and won't change anything. Reload to update it.

The version you're using is shown at the bottom of this manual.

> Moving your timeline into the app for the first time: in the copy that has it, use **More → Export a backup**, then in your app use **More → Import a file…**.
