Peloponnesian War

Wikipedia · sample text written for this prototype

The Peloponnesian War (431–404 BC) was fought between Athens and its empire, the Delian League, and Sparta with its allies in the Peloponnesian League. Historians usually divide it into three phases. Most of what we know comes from Thucydides, an Athenian general who fought in it and wrote its history.

Archidamian War

Most summers a Spartan army under King Archidamus II invaded Attica and burned its farms, while Athens, safe behind its Long Walls, sent its fleet to raid the coast of the Peloponnese. In 430 BC a plague broke out in the crowded city; it killed perhaps a quarter of the population, among them Pericles. In 425 BC, at the Battle of Sphacteria, the Athenians trapped Spartan hoplites on an island near Pylos and took 292 of them prisoner. This first phase ended with the Peace of Nicias in 421 BC.

Sicilian Expedition

In 415 BC Athens sent a great fleet against Syracuse, the most powerful Greek city on Sicily. The expedition ended in disaster in 413 BC: almost the whole force was lost, and the survivors were put to work in the city's stone quarries.

Ionian War

On the advice of the Athenian exile Alcibiades, Sparta built a permanent fort at Decelea in Attica, and Persia began to pay for a Spartan fleet. Athens could still win at sea: at the Battle of Arginusae in 406 BC its fleet defeated the Spartans, but six of its generals were then executed for failing to rescue the crews of wrecked ships. In 405 BC the Spartan admiral Lysander destroyed the Athenian fleet at Aegospotami, in the Hellespont.

Aftermath

Cut off from its grain supply, Athens surrendered in 404 BC. Its Long Walls were pulled down, its fleet was cut to twelve ships, and a pro-Spartan oligarchy, the Thirty Tyrants, took power.
