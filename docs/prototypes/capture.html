Pericles (c. 495 – 429 BC) was an Athenian statesman and general who led Athens through its golden age, between the Persian and Peloponnesian wars. He was elected general almost every year from 443 BC until his death.

Democracy

Pericles rose as an ally of Ephialtes, whose reforms in 462 BC took most of the old council's powers. In 451 BC he proposed a law that limited citizenship to men whose parents were both Athenian.

Empire and the building programme

In 454 BC the treasury of the Delian League was moved from Delos to Athens. From 447 BC Pericles used its tribute to pay for a great building programme on the Acropolis, beginning with the Parthenon, whose sculpture was overseen by Phidias. His rival Thucydides, son of Melesias, said Athens was decking itself out with the allies' money like a vain woman; in 443 BC he was ostracised.

Peloponnesian War

When the Peloponnesian War broke out in 431 BC, Pericles persuaded the Athenians to abandon the farms of Attica and shelter behind the Long Walls, trusting in the fleet. That winter he gave the funeral oration for the war's first dead, as Thucydides later wrote it down. In 430 BC plague broke out in the crowded city; Pericles died of it in the autumn of 429 BC.
