# Design notes

## Direction
A quiet study tool for one reader. The **pigment palette** is the one bold thing: each category lane takes its color from a historical pigment (lapis, cinnabar, umber, verdigris, ochre, madder, indigo). Everything else stays restrained so the history is what you see.

## Design language for the redesign (draft)
The direction is in `docs/vision.md`; visual references are in `docs/references.md`. The sections marked *proposed* are settled in the Phase 0 prototypes, then written here as rules.

- **Zoom levels.** Level 0: polity strips and world events over a backdrop of eras. Level 1: an open polity's or world event's six lanes. Level 2: individual entries. Each level shows less detail than the next, never a shrunken copy of it.
- **Representations.** Each subject type has one way of being drawn (the table in `vision.md`). Two subject types never share a glyph.
- **Density.** Era bands and theme chips never hide at level 0: a band grows taller to show all its chips. At levels 1 and 2, when crowded, hide in this order: descriptions, then secondary labels, then low-prominence entries, which merge into a cluster of their kind (see Long range below). A span label that doesn't fit sits just after the span if there's room, is cut short with an ellipsis if not, and is hidden (tooltip only) below 48 px. A moment's label hides if it would collide with the one before it.
- **Lane pigments**: Politics and law lapis, War cinnabar, Economy ochre, Philosophy and religion indigo, Technology verdigris, Nature terre verte, always in that order; gold is for world events only; umber stays for neutral rules and uncertainty.
- **Patterns** carry meaning that color can't. Every pattern is legible in dark mode and at phone size.
  - Regime forms, in lapis on polity bars and territories: one ruler solid, the few a 45° hatch, by wealth horizontal bands, the many dots. Backed by a foreign power adds an umber bar along the top.
  - How the work was done, in ochre on economy portraits and production sites: free family work plain, paid work vertical lines, enslaved work a crosshatch, a mix dots. Owned or paid by the state adds a corner notch.
- **Uncertainty** is always visible: dashed edges for approximate dates, soft bands for ranges, "size unknown" tiles, never a confident line for a guess.
- **Type scale**: Spectral SC for era and region names (spaced small caps); Spectral for theme chips, span labels, cards' titles and reading text; Public Sans for controls, lane names, moment labels and axis ticks at levels 1 and 2. The level 0 axis keeps Spectral's old-style numerals.
- **Motion tokens**: `--motion-quick` 150 ms (hover, selection), `--motion-move` 400 ms (zooming between levels, panels), `--motion-morph` tied to the year clock (borders, diagrams, treemaps), and easing `cubic-bezier(.2,.7,.2,1)`. Only change over time animates; decoration never does. With reduced motion, states swap instantly.

## Overview (level 0, and opening a polity)
Settled by Phase 4's prototype (`docs/prototypes/polities.html`); Bob chose on 2026-10-19. Build tickets match it.

- **Bands.** One band per region, its name in spaced small caps at the top left. Region parents are a small-caps rule above their regions (EUROPE, NEAR EAST AND NORTH AFRICA…). A band with no polities in the years in view is one thin row that says so.
- **Polity strips.** Polities are strips in sub-rows; a succession (Achaemenid, Seleucid, Parthia) shares a sub-row. A strip is centred on its row and as thick as its power:
  - Bob's strength points win (1–5, straight lines between points, level ÷ 5 of the row).
  - Otherwise its area to the power 0.4, so a mid-sized kingdom isn't a hairline beside an empire, scaled so the widest polity in the years in view fills its row (row height less 8 px).
  - With neither, one even width (35%) with a dotted umber edge.
  - Never thinner than 8%, and the whole row is its hit area, so a sliver can still be picked.
- **Strip look.** Fill is its region parent's tone, two shades alternating so a succession reads. Pigments belong to lanes, never to strips. An approximate start is a dashed umber line. The name sits on the strip in Spectral SC with a halo, from the strip's visible start; cut short with an ellipsis, hidden below 48 px.
- **Eras are the backdrop.** Each era is a wash across its band (two tints alternating), named in Spectral italic after the region's name; approximate edges are dashed umber. Inner eras (the Fifty Years) are a labelled rule along the band's foot when they're at least 70 px wide.
- **Groups.** A folded group is one strip as thick as its members together, "▸ Classical Greece" with the members' names after it, smaller. Clicking unfolds it into adjacent sub-rows with a bracket at the left; the bracket folds it. Both ways morph over `--motion-move`.
- **World events (B · in place).** Gold (`--gold`, a gilt gradient with an ink edge) along the top of every region the event touched: 7 px in its home region, 3 px elsewhere, a gold dot for a moment. Named on a line of its own under the home region's name. Not chosen: A · a gold row of their own across the top.
- **The top row.** Under the axis, entries Bob marks "Show in the overview" sit as their kind's glyph and name, up to three rows, most prominent first.
- **Order (A · automatic).** Region parents run west to east. Inside a parent, geography decides: the shortest path through neighbouring regions by their territories' centres. Then connections decide which ends meet where two parents join (each contact and thread counts once, plus the highlights on its entries and on what's part of them). On the Greece fixture plus the stress appendix this gives Macedon, Greece, then Persia, with China far off. Not chosen: B · rows Bob drags.
- **The year.** One year clock. Its pill on the axis drags; play moves it. When a polity is picked, each polity it dealt with in that year gets a chip at the year line: "at war" cinnabar, "allied" lapis, "trade" ochre, "at peace" umber.
- **Picking and opening.** Clicking a strip picks it (an ink outline and an "Open ▾" pill). Clicking it again, or Open, drops its six lanes beneath it over `--motion-move`. Each lane is a faint wash of its pigment, with a pill holding its name and count. A closed lane shows every entry as a faint tick and names up to five of the most prominent, never overlapping. Clicking a lane's name opens it to all its entries, packed in rows. Empty lanes say so in Bob's words ("No wars here yet."). A world event opens the same way, under its home region. Esc steps back: region focus, then lane, then the open polity, then the pick.
- **Lane pigments**, always in this order: Politics and law lapis, War cinnabar, Economy ochre, Philosophy and religion indigo, Technology verdigris, Nature terre verte. Madder retires with the social dimension. Gold is kept clear of ochre by being lighter and yellower, and by its ink edge.
- **The map follows.**
  - A picked polity: the map fits it and the polities it fought or allied with (never zooming out for a vast trade partner). It is drawn in ink, the others in their contact's colour and named with how. An alliance it led (the Delian League) is its reach, dashed lapis.
  - An open lane shows its layer. War: everyone the polity fought in the years in view, in cinnabar. Politics: its borders before, dashed. The others: pins for the lane's entries in view, stronger near the year.
  - A picked world event: the regions it touched in gold, and its entries pinned.
  - Clicking an area of the map focuses its region: its rows grow 30%, the others fade to 40%, and the map fits it. A chip with × (or Esc) clears it.
- **Phone.** Map above, timeline below, region names above their rows. An open polity is a bottom sheet: per lane, a strip of its entries, then its three most prominent as a dated list; tapping a lane lists all of it.

## Long range
Settled by the same prototype (Bob, 2026-10-19). The ideas behind it are in `vision.md` (Long range).
- **Prominence** is highlights × 3 + connections × 2 + notes × 2, plus 10 for "Show in the overview" (20 for a world event). It decides which changes a closed lane names, the top row, and which labels win when they collide.
- **Threads (A · ribbons).** On focus (hovering a strip on a laptop, tapping it on the phone), the picked polity's connections to entries in other polities roll up into one ribbon per pair of polities. A ribbon leaves the strip, runs along the gutter under the upper band, and enters the other strip. Its width is 1.4 px plus 1.1 per link (at most 9), its colour the most common lane among its links, and its label ("5 · legitimised") sits in the gutter without overlapping another. Polities the threads don't touch fade to 35%. "Show all threads" draws every ribbon at about 12%. Not chosen: B · arcs, one per connection.
- *Still proposed, for Phase 4b:* clusters (a badge in a kind's glyph with a count), threads on the map (arcs drawn in their year, then a faint trace), and lenses (thin strips under each band, never moving it).

## Influences between lanes
Settled by Phase 4b's prototype (`docs/prototypes/influences.html`); Bob chose on 2026-10-21. Ticket 2 builds it.

- **What counts.** A connection is an influence between lanes when its ends sit in different lanes. Its arrow leaves the cause's first lane (in pigment order) and lands in the effect's first lane the cause isn't in, else its first that's different (League treasury, Politics and Economy → Parthenon, Economy and Philosophy and religion: Politics to Philosophy and religion). Links inside one lane and *part of* links aren't drawn as influences. Built in Phase 4b ticket 2 (`src/ui/representations/influence.ts`, `laneInfluence` in `src/model/doc.ts`).
- **Effect vocabulary.** The editor offers, in this order: caused, enabled, prompted, funded, strengthened, weakened, legitimised, ended, then "+ your own" for a word Bob types. His words join the list for next time (an open vocabulary, edited in the app like his other ones). Read backwards, an effect becomes "Caused by", "Weakened by" and so on; a word of his own reads "⟨word⟩ by".
- **Arrows (A).** At level 1, inside an open polity or era, each influence is an arrow from the cause to what it acted on:
  - Colour: the cause's lane pigment. 1.6 px, 2.2 px in focus, with a 4.5 px halo in the background colour so it reads over labels. A filled arrowhead at the effect's end.
  - Shape: a cubic curve that leaves and enters vertically. When the ends are within about 36 px of each other, it bows sideways so it doesn't run down a column of labels.
  - Where it attaches: at the year the effect landed, which is the cause's start clamped into the effect's dates, then clamped into the cause's. Arrows sharing a span end spread along it, about 9 px apart, ordered by where their other end is.
  - Label: the effect in a small pill (Public Sans 600, 10.5 px, panel fill, outlined in the arrow's pigment) on the curve, at the middle if it's free, else further along. A label that would cover an entry's name or another label hides until its arrow is in focus.
  - An entry at either end of an arrow is always named in its lane; the rest of the lane names its three most prominent entries and ticks the others. In a closed lane the named entries sit in time order on up to three rows (neighbours on different rows), each a diamond at its start and a thin bar over its years, so an arrow lands at its year.
  - An arrow whose other end is scrolled out of view runs off the edge toward it.
- **Focus.** Hovering or focusing an arrow or an entry (laptop), or tapping one (phone), fades arrows outside it to about 16% and unrelated named entries to about 38%, over `--motion-quick`. Esc clears it.
- **The evidence is one tap away.** Clicking an arrow opens the connection: both lanes, "Decelean War *weakened* Athenian silver “owls”", Bob's note, then **Evidence**: each highlight quoted as marked in the reader, its source, and "Open in the reader". With no highlights it says "No highlights yet. Pick the passages that show this."
- **An entry's panel** lists "Effects on other lanes" both ways ("Weakened: Pericles", "Weakened by: Plague of Athens"), each line opening the connection; with none, "None yet. Link it to something in another lane and say what it did."
- **Phone.** Level 1 is the vertical list. Under each entry at an end, a line per effect: the other lane's square, the effect in bold, then the other entry ("weakened by Plague of Athens"). Arcs in the left margin join the two ends, in the cause's pigment, nested on their own tracks so they don't merge; tapping an effect line opens the connection as a bottom sheet.
- **The map** adds nothing for influences (threads across polities, ticket 4, have the map half).
- Not chosen: B · a six-by-six "what drove what" grid counting links between lanes, opening into the arrows.

## Reader and capture
- **Layout.** Laptop: the reader column (text about 34em wide) beside the map over a timeline strip; a reading trail and "Open an article" in the top bar. Phone: full-screen reader; "Map" opens the map and timeline as a sheet.
- **Highlighting.** Select any words; a "Highlight" button appears above the selection (H on the laptop) or docked at the bottom on the phone. On the phone, a tap also highlights a whole sentence, and tapping the next sentence extends it. A highlight is saved the moment it's made.
- **Capture card in a tray**, on laptop and phone alike. It slides up under the text and the reader scrolls so the highlighted passage stays above it. It shows the quote and section; what it becomes (the kind's glyph, kind and title, or "Already in Chronicle"); facts (When, Where, Part of, and anything else found); lane chips (several allowed) with Claude's suggestions marked; then "Add to map and timeline" (or "Link to …"), "Just keep the highlight" and Remove. Moving on keeps an undecided highlight as a plain one.
- **Where facts came from.** Wikidata facts are plain; Claude's suggestions are dashed and say "suggested"; links to entries already in Chronicle are underlined in the pigment. The card says which applies.
- **Drafts.** Until added, a record shows on the map and timeline as a dashed ghost with a "?". Adding it pops the pin in with one ring; nothing else animates.
- **Marks in the text.** The open highlight: a 32% wash of its first lane's pigment (umber with none); kept: 13%; added: 20% plus a pigment underline.
- **Glyphs**, one per kind, declared by each kind's module in `src/ui/representations/` (the tray, editor and timeline all draw from there): war, a bar with ticks (its battles are ticks on the timeline bar); battle, a circled ×; event, a dot; person, a lifeline (a hollow ring on the map); change of government, a hatched square; polity, a solid square; alliance, two rings. Bob picked the rest on 2026-10-18 from the kind glyphs sheet: period, an open bar (today's span bar); movement or idea, a four-pointed spark on a thin thread; technology, a cog; economy, a stack of coins at the start of its bar; work, a page with a folded corner, tied to its maker's lifeline; migration, chevrons, on a bar ending in an arrow point; place, a pin with a dot standing on a thin ground line. On the timeline a moment of a kind with a glyph is the glyph on a panel-coloured disc (dashed when approximate); a bar of a kind with a badge (polity, economy, migration, technology, work, battle) starts with its glyph.
- **The tray in the app** (Phase 1, 2026-10-17): it sticks to the bottom of the reader column. With nothing open it is one line counting the article's highlights. Until highlights become records (Phase 2), "Becomes" is the article's own subject, and the picker has no suggestion yet. A highlight already linked to the subject shows "Open …" and "Done".
- **Becomes** (Phase 2; ticks since Phase 4 ticket 3). The tray lists every candidate: the article's own subject first, then the pages the passage links, de-duplicated, in reading order. One line each: a tick box, glyph, title, kind (and "This article" for the subject), "In Chronicle" (saying what it is there, such as "as a territory" or "Parthenon built") or "Linked". Any number can be ticked. A ticked row is tinted in the highlight's pigment, its tick filled in that pigment, and its facts sit under it; each ticked row not in Chronicle has its dashed ghost on the map. A row that can't be ticked yet has a dashed box and "can't be added yet", and clicking it says why; linked rows stay ticked, with "Open" beside them. The main button follows the ticks: "Link to …" or "Add to map and timeline" (the editor) for one, "Add N to map and timeline" for several, which adds them at once through the same path as Claude's accepted drafts, with one Undo. The first ticks (`defaultChoices`) are what it's on; else the one ticked last; else the first event, war or battle the passage links; else the article's subject, unless it can't be added. The last row, "Something else…" (a dashed plus), opens a small form under the list (title, event / period / person, dates, place, lanes), saved the same way; what it made shows as a linked row. An idea not in Chronicle says why it can't be added and offers "Ask Claude" instead (places became addable in Phase 3 ticket 4, polities in Phase 4 ticket 4). A polity is never ticked first; adding one reads the historical maps for its borders by name ("Looking for Sparta's borders…"), and when they aren't there the card holds the add with a notice and "Draw its borders" / "Add without borders yet". This is a stopgap: every kind will be drawn on both the map and the timeline (decision of 2026-10-17), and each kind becomes addable once its representation lands. Once anything uses the highlight, "Just keep the highlight" becomes "Done".
- **Ask Claude** sits in the tray under the lane chips, labelled "for what has no article of its own", with a dashed umber button. Offline it says "Asking Claude needs a connection."; with no key, "Add your Anthropic API key in Drafting with AI first." While it works: "Claude is reading this passage and its section…".
- **Claude's drafts sit in the tray** (not today's draft panel), under "Entries" and "Connections", and the tray scrolls to them when they arrive. Each row is a dashed umber card: glyph, title, kind · date · place ("looked up on Wikidata"), then the passage's words it rests on in quotes, then Accept, Edit (entries only) and Reject. A connection reads "from ⟨effect⟩ to", with the effect in a dashed pill; ends in Chronicle are underlined in the pigment, draft ends dashed. A connection to a draft entry stays disabled with "Accept the entry first." Accepted rows turn solid ("Added · on the map and timeline", Undo); rejected ones fade and are struck through ("Left out", Undo). Records already in Chronicle aren't drafted again, and the tray says so. The rows are the same component as `DraftReview`'s.
- **Lane chips** (tray and editor): a pill per lane in pigment order, a pigment square and the lane's name; picked ones fill with a 14% wash of the pigment and a pigment edge. **Suggested lanes** get a dashed umber ring and "Claude suggests Economy · pick it to use it" under the chips. A suggestion is never taken until Bob picks it; until then the highlight stays as it was.
- **Drafts on the map and timeline**: waiting entries are dashed umber ghosts with a "?"; drafted connections are dashed umber arrows on the timeline labelled with the effect and a "?" (a loop to the left when both ends share a year), solid once accepted. Pins at one place stack into a list beside it.
- **Folded infobox.** Under `ui.next` the infobox starts as one line: its picture as a 34 px thumbnail, "Infobox · picture and N rows", and "Show ⌄". Open, it shows the picture with its caption beside the rows (stacked on the phone) and reads "Hide". The choice is remembered per device, and its rows can be highlighted.

## Wars on the map
- **Sides** take their alliance's colour (the sample: Delian League lapis, Peloponnesian League cinnabar). Cities are polity squares in the colour of the side they're on that year, an outline when neutral; a fort is an outlined square.
- **Campaign routes, Minard style.** A route is as wide as its force, in men (a trireme carried about 200): 22 px for 40,000 on the laptop, 0.6× on the phone, never under 1.6 px. A force that changes along the way tapers point by point (the retreat from Syracuse, 40,000 to 7,000). When sources disagree, a soft band at the highest estimate sits behind a solid core at the lowest. An unknown size is a 1.6 px dashed line. The head carries an arrow and, while it draws, the route's name and size.
- **Routes draw in** between their dates on the year clock, then fade to about a quarter over a year and a half and stay, so the end of the war is a map of the whole of it.
- **Battles** are a circled × in the winner's colour that rings out once (two rings, about a second) when the year reaches it; no ring with reduced motion. Recent battles are labelled, older ones on hover. Clicking one opens a card: kind, title, season, outcome, what happened, the highlights behind it.
- **Playback is steady**: an even pace (0.7 years a second at 1×, with ½× and 2×), showing the whole war. While a route draws in, the year eases so its name and size can be read, then resumes the even pace (2026-10-17, after the map spike). A border change morphs over about 0.7 years of the clock, centred on the new snapshot's year. On the laptop that is Sicily to the Hellespont; on the phone, Greece and the Aegean, with "All" for the rest. Drag pans, the wheel or a pinch zooms.
- **The war's bar** under the map is the war glyph at full size: its phases as segments (a peace lighter and in italics), a tick per battle in the winner's colour, and a playhead to drag. The year card counts summers and winters, as Thucydides does: "Summer of 425 BCE · Year 7 of 27 · Archidamian War".

## Polities: how they were governed
- **Bodies.** Each institution is a circle whose area is its number of members: offices at the top, councils and courts in the middle, assemblies at the bottom. Each keeps its place across regimes, so a change reads as circles growing, shrinking, appearing and going. A soft ring reaches to the high end of a range; a dashed circle means the number is unknown; a faint outline is an institution in name only; a foreign power is umber, at the side.
- **Links** between them: an arrow for elects or chooses, an open arrow for prepares business for, a bar end for checks or can overturn, a heavy arrow for commands, each labelled ("by lot", "can strike down decrees"). Two links between one pair sit side by side.
- **Changes** morph over about a second when the year crosses one, and Play holds there for a beat. Reduced motion swaps them.
- **Who had a say**: one bar of everyone who lived there, on a scale shared by every polity and year: could vote in lapis, free without a vote in a lapis wash, enslaved in grey, a faint band for the range of the total, and a black tick and label for the few who held power. Before there are numbers, a dashed outline says so.
- **The polity's bar** is split into its regimes, patterned by form, with a dashed "not read yet" after the last one Bob has read about. Arrows step between regimes; the card says what changed and shows the highlights.
- **Side by side**: two polities in one year on the same scale (Athens and Sparta in 431 BCE), then a short table of who could vote, how officials were chosen, who led the army and who could stop a decision. On the phone they stack.

## Economies
- **The portrait is a treemap.** A tile's area is the sector's share of the people working (the middle of its range), and its texture says how the work was done. Farming is a column and the other sectors sit in rows, always in the same order, so snapshots morph over about two-thirds of a second without tiles jumping. A tile shows its name, its range ("38–55%") and who did the work while they fit; a sector nobody can size is a dashed tile with "?", never a guess. The portrait says which snapshot it shows, and holds it until the next.
- **The economic lane is a stream** whose thickness is the estimated number of people, so the economy swells and shrinks; layers keep the sector order, alternate two ochre tints with a gap between them, and are labelled where thickest. Umber lines mark the snapshots the shape is drawn from; diamonds mark moments; after the last snapshot, a dashed "not read yet".
- **Goods** move as dots along their route: filled, on a band as wide as the amount, when the amount is known; hollow on a dashed line when it isn't. A cut route turns grey and dashed with a ⊘ where it was cut. Migrations keep their moving dashes, so the two never look alike.
- **Production sites** are squares in their sector's texture; an idle one is a dashed outline. A home region too small for the main map gets an inset (Attica, with the Long Walls while they stand). Picking a sector lights up its sites and its layer and shows its highlights.
- **Moments** where the economy turns (the silver strike, Decelea, the grain cut off) hold Play for a beat and show their highlight in the year card.

## Tokens (in `src/ui/styles.css`)
- Light: limestone background `#eef0ec`, panel `#fbfbf8`, slate ink `#1d2b3a`, rules `#cfd4cc`.
- Dark: `#131b23` background with lightened pigments. It follows the system setting.
- Type: **Spectral** for titles, notes and anything you read (axis years use old-style numerals), and **Public Sans** for controls.

## Conventions
- Approximate dates are shown with a dashed edge, not a "c." prefix that eats label space.
- Each trend strip has its own scale, and the label says when a strip is log scale.
- The drawer is a side panel on desktop and a bottom sheet under 860px. Its back stack lets you follow links (note → entry → source) and return.
- Copy: sentence case, verbs that say what happens, offline errors that say a connection is needed.

## Map
- Parchment land on slate-blue water with a faint graticule; dark mode inverts to near-black water. Tokens: `--map-water`, `--map-land`, `--map-border`, `--map-river`, `--map-backdrop`.
- Territories take their category's pigment at 30% fill with a solid edge, labelled in spaced small caps (Spectral). Reference borders are dashed ink outlines, deliberately quieter than Bob's own territories.
- Pins are category-colored dots, bigger when selected. Events fade over a few decades after they happen (longer when playing fast). Names show when 40 or fewer pins are visible, and on hover otherwise.
